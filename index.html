How to use this

Mentee

Work one topic at a time

  1. Open a topic and read what the syllabus asks first
  2. Read the notes underneath, then close them and say the topic aloud or write it down on a piece of paper from memory.
  3. Name your case study for that topic. If you can't give a place, a figure and a date, you don't have one yet.
  4. Answer the self-check questions to the mark allocation, then mark learning or confident. Watch your cross-section rise.

Mentor

Run a 40-minute session

  1. 5 min — open with the recall prompt. Let them talk before you correct anything.
  2. 10 min — work the "check they can" list. Anything they stumble on becomes the session.
  3. 15 min — one exam question, timed, to the mark allocation.
  4. 10 min — mark it together, then agree on the status. Be honest!

Paper 2

Map and graph skills

Answers to the mapwork question must come from map evidence only. Work through this list with a real 1:50 000 extract, not from memory.

On the map extract
  • Four- and six-figure grid references — first tenth is 0, last is 9.
  • Direction as a 16-point compass bearing and as a bearing from grid north (bring a protractor).
  • Distance with a straight paper edge against the scale line; break curves into straight sections.
  • Height difference by contour reading; interpret or complete a cross-section.
  • Describe relief and drainage: ridge, scarp, plateau, flood plain; broad, steep-sided, gently sloping.
  • Settlement pattern — dispersed, nucleated, linear — plus site, growth and function.
  • Communications: type and density in relation to relief.
Graphs and data you must read
  • Population pyramids: compare male/female, working and dependent groups.
  • Line, bar, divided bar, histogram, pictogram, pie.
  • Scatter graphs with best-fit lines; positive and negative relationships.
  • Triangular graphs — employment structure.
  • Wind-rose, radial, kite, dispersion graphs.
  • Isoline maps and choropleth maps.
  • Plot onto given axes accurately, and suggest a suitable graph for a data set.

Paper 4 mark levels, in plain terms

Five criteria, 12 marks each, three levels — the shape of Paper 4's mark allocation.

CriterionLevel 1 (1–4)Level 2 (5–8)Level 3 (9–12)
Knowledge with understandingSimple terms, tentative grasp of the aimsAppropriate terms, clear link between aims and geographyComprehensive, careful terminology, aims tied to key ideas
Observation and collection of dataBasic information, limited sources, simple planningRelevant information, valid sources, sound planningDetailed data, range of valid sources, clear planning design
Organisation and presentationLoosely ordered, one simple techniqueLogically ordered, two or more techniques used wellCoherent, a range of techniques, accurate and relevant
Analysis and interpretationDescriptive, simple commentsValid statements, some explanation attemptedThorough interpretation with reasoned explanation
Conclusion and evaluationSuperficial conclusions, little awareness of shortcomingsTentative conclusions linked to aims, some evaluationClear conclusions from evidence, weaknesses identified and improved
